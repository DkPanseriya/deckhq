Privacy and security

DeckHQ runs on your machine and stays there. Everything it knows, it read off your own disk. Everything it keeps, it wrote to your own disk. Nothing goes anywhere else.

What we never do

  • Send your work anywhere. No analytics, no telemetry, no update check, no crash reports. There is no setting to turn off, because there is nothing to turn off.
  • Ask who you are. No account, no sign-in, no licence key. Every feature is there on the first run.
  • Change your Claude Code settings behind your back. DeckHQ reads your transcripts. It writes to your settings file only after you have seen the exact lines and clicked, and it removes only what it put there.
  • Open a door on your network. DeckHQ listens on your own machine and nowhere else. A phone on the same wifi cannot reach it, and neither can a page in another browser tab.

Where your data lives

In one folder: ~/.deckhq/ on macOS and Linux, C:\Users\you\.deckhq\ on Windows. Your queue, the names on the floor, your settings, a cache so a restart is quick, and the local token ledger. Delete that folder and there is nothing left of DeckHQ on the machine. Set DECKHQ_STATE_DIR to keep it somewhere else.

Your conversations are never copied into it. DeckHQ reads the transcript files your coding tool already writes, shows you the last thing each session said, and leaves them where they are.

What you can share

If you report a problem, deckhq doctor --share prints a report you can paste into an issue with everything personal taken out first: no file paths, no project names, no machine name, and the date to the day rather than the hour.

Reporting a vulnerability

Please do not open a public issue for a security problem. The reporting route, and what to expect after you use it, is in SECURITY.md. Private reports can also go through the security tab on the repository.

Anything else — a bug, a wrong number, a runtime DeckHQ reads badly — belongs in issues.

This site

The page you are reading keeps the same promise as the product. No web fonts, no CDN, no analytics, no cookies, no third-party anything. It loads a stylesheet, two small scripts and its pictures, all from this address, and it stores one thing in your browser: whether you asked for the light or the dark version.
