The characters.

A session on the floor is a person at a desk. Its colour is its state, its face is its identity, and both have to read at 22 pixels from across the room.

The state owns the whole body.

Six states, and they are the only thing on the floor colour is spent on. An agent can never wear a state colour as identity, and identity can never borrow one: every avatar colour is held a fixed distance from all six, checked rather than eyeballed.

  • Working Typing. Producing output right now.
  • Needs input A raised hand. Blocked on a question only you can answer.
  • Stalled Slumped. Silent longer than the stall window.
  • For review Finished its turn and standing in your office, waiting on a reply.
  • Benched You have dealt with it. In the lounge, out of the queue.
  • Let go Gone from disk. On its way off the floor.

The names are the model, not the colour: a state is carried by a pose and a label as well as by a hue, so the floor is legible to a reader who sees neither. The model in 60 seconds is the whole of it.

One figure, drawn from the session id.

Identity is a face, a name and sometimes a hat, derived from the session id, so the same session is the same person on every restart. The name comes from a pool of 600 and is kept for good.

A junior is the same figure, smaller, standing beside its parent and named after it. The bright visor is the most findable element on the floor, which is what makes a room of six scannable at a glance.

Twelve ways it moves.

Typing at a screen, a thinking cloud, standing up, walking, running, sitting down, and the lounge activities a benched agent fills its time with. Every phase comes from one injected clock, so two renders at the same instant are the same picture.

Under prefers-reduced-motion each clip holds one representative pose and nothing moves, with a golden proving the floor still reads.

What a benched agent does with its time.

The lounge has four bays. A session you have dealt with leaves your office for one of them and stays on the floor, out of the queue and off the count.

It is the part of the building nobody is asking you for anything from, which is why it is the part worth looking at when the office is clear.

Coming: the crew.

A session that spawns sub-agents should show them as a crew: three or more live sub-agents drawn smaller around their parent, each with a laptop, joined by cables carrying pulses back to the parent desk. Not built. Today sub-agents are juniors beside their parent, without the cables.

Coming: agent size.

One figure size ships. A setting for small to large, with the desks and chairs scaling with the figures and the room rectangles staying exactly where they are, is designed and not built.

The mark

DeckHQ’s own icon lives once, as a hand-written SVG — six rounded rectangles, both grounds in one file, no font and no external reference — and every PNG, the favicon.ico and the VS Code tile are rendered from it by a script that rasterises the shapes itself rather than driving a browser. A test compares the committed files against a fresh render on every run, so the source and its outputs cannot drift apart.

The one colour in it is the amber on the antenna tip, and it is the accent this site is built on.
