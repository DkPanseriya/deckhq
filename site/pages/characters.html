The characters.

A session on the floor is a person at a desk. Its colour is its state, its face is its identity, and both have to read at 22 pixels from across the room.

The state owns the whole body.

Six states, and they are the only thing on the floor colour is spent on. An agent can never wear a state colour as identity, and identity can never borrow one: every avatar colour is held a fixed distance from all six, checked rather than eyeballed.

  • Working Typing. Producing output right now.
  • Needs input A raised hand. Blocked on a question only you can answer.
  • Stalled Slumped. Silent longer than the stall window.
  • For review Finished its turn and standing in your office, waiting on a reply.
  • Benched You have dealt with it. In the lounge, out of the queue.
  • Let go Gone from disk. On its way off the floor.

A state is carried by a pose and a label as well as by a colour, so the floor still reads if you see colour differently or not at all.

One figure, drawn from the session id.

Identity is a face, a name and sometimes a hat, derived from the session id, so the same session is the same person on every restart. The name comes from a pool of 600 and is kept for good.

A junior is the same figure, smaller, standing beside its parent and named after it. The bright visor is the most findable element on the floor, which is what makes a room of six scannable at a glance.

Twelve ways it moves.

Typing at a screen, a thinking cloud, standing up, walking, running, sitting down, and the lounge activities a benched agent fills its time with.

If your system asks for reduced motion, the floor holds still: every figure keeps a pose that says what it is doing, and nothing moves.

What a benched agent does with its time.

The lounge has four bays. A session you have dealt with leaves your office for one of them and stays on the floor, out of the queue and off the count.

It is the part of the building nobody is asking you for anything from, which is why it is the part worth looking at when the office is clear.

A session with a crew of its own.

When a session sets three or more sub-agents working, its desk becomes a formation: the juniors seated in an arc in front of it with a laptop each, and a cable from every laptop back to the desk.

  • A pulse means a file is moving

    A cable pulses while that junior is still writing, and goes grey when it stops. It tells you something is happening, and never pretends to know how far along it is.

  • Twelve, then a count

    Twelve juniors are drawn around the desk and the rest become a small chip. Every one of them is still in the panel and in the table, so nobody is lost to the drawing.

  • No cable without a link

    A cable is a claim that one session started another. Where that link is guessed rather than reported, the junior keeps its seat and gets no cable.

As big or as small as you like.

Agent size is a setting: small, medium, large, or automatic. The desks, chairs, sofas and rugs resize with the people, and the rooms stay where they are, so a floor of five fills the window and a floor of a hundred still fits in it.

The look has the rest of what you can change.

The mark

DeckHQ’s icon is one hand-drawn SVG: six rounded rectangles, no font, nothing fetched from anywhere. The one colour in it is the amber on the antenna tip, and that amber is the accent this site is built on.
