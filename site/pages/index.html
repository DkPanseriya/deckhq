Every AI coding session on your machine, on one office floor.

DeckHQ draws each session as a robot at a desk. You see who is working, who is waiting on you, and for how long.

npx deckhq app

See what it does

Node 18 or newer. Free, MIT, zero dependencies, and it stays on your machine.

A session that finished is still on the floor.

An agent list shows what is running. DeckHQ reads every transcript on disk, so the session that finished an hour ago is still here, with what it last said.

One room per repository. One robot per session.

Start your agents anywhere: a terminal, your editor, the desktop app. Each one turns up in its project's room with a first name it keeps for good.

The plate on the room says what you might act on in the biggest type: 2 need you · oldest 1d 2h. Under it, what somebody is running right now, and today's tokens.

Everyone waiting on you is in your office.

A session that finishes its turn stands up, walks the corridor into Your Office and sits down. How long it has waited is over its head, and the longest wait sits nearest your desk.

Two signals, because they need different answers.

  • A raised hand means I am mid-task and blocked. It needs a decision.
  • A red badge with a time means I finished; review this. It needs a read.
  • They are counted separately. The header reads 2 hands up · 4 for review.

Reading it does not clear it. Only you do.

What a session is doing changes on its own. What you owe it changes when you press a button. Opening a conversation does not clear it; scrolling past it does not clear it; reading it does not clear it.

Click anyone and the panel has how long they have waited, what they said as the markdown they wrote, and what changed in that project's working tree. Then 1 reply, 2 approve, 3 bench.

Go to session brings the terminal, the editor or the Claude desktop app that session is running in to the front. It is measured on Windows 11, and written and not yet run on macOS and Linux.

The panel, in full

The details people notice.

Everything on the floor is a session that exists on your disk. Nothing is animated to look busy.

A lead with a crew.

A session that starts three or more sub-agents turns its desk into a formation: juniors cross-legged in an arc, a laptop each, and a cable to the desk that pulses while that junior is still writing. Twelve are drawn; beyond that a +N chip.

Juniors say so.

A sub-agent is drawn one size smaller than the session that started it, under a two-row tag: a Junior chip over its name.

A worktree is a bench, and the branch is written on it.

Five checkouts of one repository are one room. The main checkout has the desks in the middle; each git worktree somebody is working in is a bench against the wall.

The lights go off.

When every session of a project is waiting in your office, its room keeps one empty desk and goes dark until one of them goes back to work.

Eleven styles, one click apart.

Look in the header repaints the whole floor: materials, furniture, partitions and light. Your agents keep their colours, because colour is state.

All eleven, and the light

It listens on 127.0.0.1 and nothing else.

There is no --host flag and there never will be one. No account, no analytics, no telemetry, no update check, no crash report, no font or script from a CDN.

Before you install anything, npx deckhq doctor prints what is on your disk. Its last line is the one to read.

What it never does, and how to check

$ npx deckhq doctor
  claude code     available
  transcripts     77 sessions across 20 projects

One command.

It starts DeckHQ, opens the floor in a window of its own, and asks once whether you want a desktop icon.

npx deckhq app

Needs Node 18 or newer. Nothing else: the package has no dependencies.

No Node on the machine

One line checks for Node, offers to install it, installs DeckHQ and opens the window. Both scripts are short files you can read first.

irm https://dkpanseriya.github.io/deckhq/install.ps1 | iex

curl -fsSL https://dkpanseriya.github.io/deckhq/install.sh | sh

A step at a time

Install it, open it, then add the icon when you want one.

npm install -g deckhq

deckhq app
